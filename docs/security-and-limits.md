## 8. Security and limits

### Risks and limitations

| Risk | What it means | Mitigation | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Star graphs | Real x402 payment graphs are star-shaped, so debt cycles are rare and little offsets. Netting and clearing remove little there (ADR-16). | The demo circle is closed and dense, and the metrics show relief against graph density, including a star graph. See section 9. | Limitation. Relief on star graphs stays near zero. |
| Dense circles clear only part of the debt per epoch | `submit_clearing` takes at most 300 edges (K-44), and the solver drops whole cycles to fit. The solver benchmark on synthetic graphs (`solver/docs/benchmark.md`, SV-019; no real payment graph was measured) shows the cost: a random circle of 100 members with 2,909 obligations would clear 90.6% of the debt with the full solution but 14.7% after the cut, and 200 members with 12,130 obligations 92.4% but 3.8%. | The cut keeps whole cycles, so what is cleared is always valid. A circle whose solution fits in 300 edges is not cut (the benchmark case of 50 members with 238 obligations gives 176 edges and clears 61.0% of the debt). | Limitation. Large dense circles clear a fraction per epoch until a clearing can span several transactions (not built). |
| Clearing could block the epoch (CT-098, CT-099) | On the deployed interface 0.0.12, `commit_epoch` reverts when the logs of its receipt pass 16,384 bytes (`TotalLogLengthExceeded`): a proposal of 210 edges already failed in the sandbox, although `submit_clearing` takes 300. A large proposal that was accepted could then stall the epoch. | Interface 0.0.13 (CT-099) makes the events of `commit_epoch` and `finalize_epoch` a fixed size (a hash of the edges, the positions in full); the edges are read from a view and from `list_obligations`. The golden vector, the SDK parser and hashes (SD-080) and the indexer (BE-077) follow it. Until the contract is deployed, keep proposals below the 210 edges measured. | Open. 0.0.13 is not finished or deployed; the testnet and the release v0.0.11 are 0.0.12 and older. |
| Credit risk | Credit that exceeds collateral can default. | The hackathon version is economically fully collateralized: `limit ≤ stake` for every member at all times (ADR-18). The limit grows only from cash settlement. For the later phase with `limit > stake`, the loss order is stake, then reserve fund, then haircut to the direct creditors of the debtor, with no cascade. | MVP: invariant in the contract. Roadmap: true undercollateralized credit. |
| Public ledger | Who trades with whom is visible on-chain. | Accepted for the MVP. | Roadmap: confidential settlement with Confidential Intents (ADR-15). Not in the MVP. |
| Dependency on NEAR Intents | A fault in Intents affects the withdrawal to other chains. The deposit incident of 1 Oct 2026 is the context for this risk. | Intents is used only for the residual payment and the creditor withdrawal. Clearing runs in the x207 contract and does not depend on Intents (ADR-01, ADR-02). | MVP: separation in place. Cross-chain withdrawal is affected if Intents has a problem. |
| Cash collusion | Members can trade for cash to raise limits. | Cycle clearing does not count as history, stake is required, and the limit has a cap (`limit_cap`). | Limitation. Collusion paid in cash stays possible within the cap. |
| Real demand is not proven | Agents do not yet pay each other at scale. x207 reduces the value that has to move between agents (not their gas on NEAR, which is higher) but does not create demand. | The demo shows value moved less, not volume. | Open. Needs a dense community of agents. |
| Competitors | 4Mica could add multi-party aggregation. Others can copy the mechanism. | x207 follows the official `batch-settlement` scheme and uses an open solver and on-chain verification. | Open. |
| Goods and services are not judged (F5, F9) | A bundle fixes who pays whom, not whether the goods or services were delivered or good. | The value counts only after every party signs the `terms_hash`. Each leg is a voucher with a cap, so a payer loses at most its leg, and an unpaid residual follows the default model of F6. There is no dispute process. | Limitation. Quality and disputes are outside the protocol. |
| Listing spam and seeker abuse (F9) | A seeker could flood agents with offers, or a listing could be a trap. | The seeker visits only agents that are `discoverable`, idle and opted in; the seeker share cannot exceed the discount; the book allows one pending referral per seeker, target and listing and at most five pending per agent; a refused pair is never offered again; a seeker must be an active member with stake. Class `X` assets are rejected. | MVP: guards in the code. No test at scale. |
| The listing book is off-chain (F9) | The book, the negotiation and the referral inbox run in the backend (M11). An operator could hide or reorder listings. | The book cannot create a deal: the contract recomputes `terms_hash` from the legs and requires every participant's signature, and the formats are public (`sdk/spec/marketplace.md`) so another book can serve the same circle. | Limitation. One book per circle in the hackathon version; it is not decentralized. |
| Bundle headroom is locked (F5) | A signed bundle reserves the headroom of each payer for its legs during the epoch. A payer that signs and never pays blocks only its own headroom. | The reservation is per epoch and per payer, it is released as the legs are registered, and a payee that did not sign cannot be bound. | Limitation. Reserved headroom cannot be used for other payments in that epoch. |
| NFT escrow (F8 class A) | The circle contract holds an NFT for sale. A failed transfer could leave a payment without an asset, or an asset that nobody can take back. | The buyer's voucher and the transfer are one flow: if `nft_transfer` fails the registration is taken back exactly (pair, debt, totals, edge) and the escrow is `held` again. `revoke_grant` is refused while the escrow holds the asset, a pair locked by an escrow takes no other voucher, and the grantor takes the NFT back with `cancel_escrow` after the grant expires. If the registration cannot be taken back (the pair or the epoch moved) the escrow is `stuck` and the NFT stays in the circle. | Limitation. NFT only, mock NFT contract in `near-sandbox`, not on testnet or mainnet. The `stuck` state exists but no test reaches it. |
| Rented access and delegated secrets (F8 classes B and C) | A leaked function-call key or a broker that holds a provider secret could be abused. | Class B: the key is restricted to the receiver, the methods and the allowance of the grant, the NEAR protocol refuses anything else, and the broker deletes it on expiry or when the lessee defaults. Class C: the lessee never receives the secret, every call is checked against the scope and the quota of the grant and is paid with a voucher before it is settled. | Limitation. Class C has no TEE and no attestation: the secret is in the environment of the broker, so the broker operator can read it. Only a mock provider has been tried. |

Seller delay risk is limited to the vouchers not yet registered on-chain (ADR-23). Secrets live only in `.env`, which is not committed.

### Impact, security, and sustainability

**Impact and volume.** Real agent payment volume is small. Reports on x402 say the same: Chainalysis counted more than 100 million agentic transactions on Base up to Q1 2026 and concluded that mass adoption remains distant. Analyses that filter wash trading and non-agent payments (a16z, TRM Labs) reduce the headline figures sharply. A study on arXiv (2607.12575) finds that settlement counts measure how easily transactions can be produced, not adoption. Payment graphs in practice are star-shaped and have few cycles, so the relief from clearing there is close to zero (ADR-16). x207 does not create demand. It lowers the cost of small payments between agents that already trade with each other. Savings figures in this README come from the relief curve in section 9 and from the demo epoch once it has run. Neither gives a market estimate.

**Barter and marketplace (F5, F9).** Barter is a way to use the same credit and clearing for goods and services, not a source of volume. The demo figures in [sandbox-demo.md](sandbox-demo.md) are small on purpose (a few cents per leg) and come from a sandbox. The seeker and the discount are an incentive design: the seeker share and the cashback are legs of the same bundle, so they are cleared and paid like any other debt, and the agent that lists carries the discount. Whether agents will list and negotiate at scale is not shown. The value of a deal is what the parties sign; the protocol does not judge quality (see the risk table).

**Reviews.** The facilitator and the cache of its chain reads were reviewed as an attacker (`tests/security-review-facilitator.test.ts`, [backend/docs/security-review-2.md](https://github.com/x207-labs/backend/blob/main/docs/security-review-2.md)): a voucher used by parallel requests is now served once (`voucher_in_flight`), and the staleness of the cache and its cost are written down ([sdk/docs/rpc-load.md](https://github.com/x207-labs/sdk/blob/main/docs/rpc-load.md)). The contract was reviewed for the features F5 to F8 (`contracts/docs/security-review.md`). The frontend was reviewed for what data of other users can do in the browser and for what the voucher key can be made to sign ([frontend/docs/security-review.md](https://github.com/x207-labs/frontend/blob/main/docs/security-review.md)). These are our own reviews, not an audit. The list of findings, their status and the command that repeats each check is one page: [docs/security.md](security.md). The interface of the contract, version by version (0.0.1 to 0.0.9), with the releases that are reproducible and those that are not, is in [CHANGELOG.md](../CHANGELOG.md). The 0.0.9 contract was read again by a second reader who did not write it ([docs/security-review-3.md](security-review-3.md)): the five questions about the Intents deposit, the withdrawal and `prune_epoch` have an answer, and five findings (a contract-paid storage cost per intent, first-come intent hashes, no event on `remove_intent`, intents and grants left open by `exit`) were reported to the contract owner with failing tests.

**Reproducible release.** The wasm of a release is built twice in a pinned container (image by digest, `cargo near build non-reproducible-wasm --locked`, `Cargo.lock` committed) and nothing is published if the two sha256 differ. The first reproducible release is **`v0.0.9`**: the wasm in its `SHA256SUMS` is `fa8309059a84…` (the file has the full value; `shasum -a 256 -c SHA256SUMS` passes), built twice with the same result on the runner of the release workflow and twice in our own container run (CT-087), so the `code_hash` of an account on chain can be traced to a tag; [contracts/docs/verify-release.md](https://github.com/x207-labs/contracts/blob/main/docs/verify-release.md) shows how to check it. Limits: the repository is private, so a third-party verifier such as SourceScan may compute another number until it is public; v0.0.8 and older were built before the pinned container and are not reproducible; the rehearsals and the mainnet run use the wasm of a release and record its `code_hash` ([docs/mainnet-runbook.md](mainnet-runbook.md)). The next release, **`v0.0.11`** (tag on `be4da8f`, interface 0.0.10 and 0.0.11), is reproducible too: wasm sha256 `a9d0c839149a6e84ce4f87c1898b2cf871d2eda117f1988e13d0c59ae13a2f39`, `code_hash` `CRtYqTc47ADcDkHp7UJaRPLXu5XHS7KYftrTNUnZ8bNx` ([release](https://github.com/x207-labs/contracts/releases/tag/v0.0.11)).

### Keys of an agent

An agent holds two kinds of key, and they do different work.

| Key | What it signs | What it can do | If it leaks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Voucher key** | Vouchers, listings and offers (ed25519, verified in the contract against `pubkey_voucher`). A separate key: it is no access key of any NEAR account, so it cannot sign a transaction | Nothing outside x207. It cannot transfer funds, NEAR or tokens, and it cannot withdraw the stake | Vouchers up to the credit limit of the member (`limit ≤ stake`), so at most the stake that is already locked. `request_exit` from the NEAR account makes the facilitator refuse new vouchers ([sdk/docs/voucher-key-security.md](https://github.com/x207-labs/sdk/blob/main/docs/voucher-key-security.md)) |
| **NEAR access key, full access** | Transactions of the agent account: `join` (deposit 0.35 NEAR, not returned), the stake and the residual (`ft_transfer_call`, 1 yoctoNEAR) and `claim` (1 yoctoNEAR) | Anything the account can do | Everything on that account: the balance of the agent account is the largest possible loss |

Why a full-access key at all: `join`, the stake, `settle` and `claim` attach a deposit, and the NEAR protocol does not let a function-call key attach one. The MCP server therefore reads the permission of the key before it sends (`view_access_key`); with a function-call key these tools refuse and send nothing, and with a full-access key they run only when the operator sets `X207_ALLOW_FULL_ACCESS_KEY=1` (and `X207_ALLOW_OUTFLOWS=1` for `join` and `settle`). Both switches are off by default, and then the server holds only the voucher key: paying, listing, offering, accepting and confirming never need a NEAR key (`sdk/packages/mcp/test/write.test.ts` TC-SD-082-02 and TC-SD-082-09). The full-access key is held by the owner of the agent, on a dedicated account that holds only what the agent may lose; the session budget and the price cap protect against a model that misbehaves, not against someone who holds the key ([sdk/packages/mcp/README.md](https://github.com/x207-labs/sdk/blob/main/packages/mcp/README.md)). The `exact` scheme for a buyer outside any circle also needs a full-access key: it signs a `SignedDelegateAction` for one `ft_transfer`, and function-call keys are rejected. The facilitator relays vouchers and cannot forge one (ADR-06).

This is a limit of today's design, not a claim of safety: the contract owner account of `x207-v013.testnet` is a Sputnik DAO, but the contract account itself still has one full-access key, a decision that rests with the owner (KN-18).

**The 207 report is outside the x402 cycle.** The report is a read endpoint of the backend. It does not take part in `402`, `PAYMENT-SIGNATURE`, or `PAYMENT-RESPONSE`, and it is not x402 wire behaviour (ADR-10).

**Loss order.** If a debtor does not pay, the loss is covered in this order: the stake of the debtor, then the reserve fund, then a haircut to the direct creditors of the debtor, with no cascade (ADR-18). In the hackathon version `limit ≤ stake`, so the stake covers the whole amount.

**Who pays the clearing fee, and where it goes.** `fee_total = cleared_total × (clearing_fee_bps + solver_fee_bps) / 10000`. It is charged to members pro rata to their cleared outflow and taken from their stake. The `clearing_fee_bps` part goes to the reserve fund. The `solver_fee_bps` part goes to the fee balance of the winning solver. Sputnik DAO sets both parameters with `set_params`, each within 0–100 bps. Both default to 0 in the contract. The circle `x207-v013.testnet` ran `clearing_fee_bps` 10 and `solver_fee_bps` 5 until epoch 47 and runs 0 and 0 since epoch 48 (DAO proposal 5, [governance-live.md](governance-live.md)); `get_params` returned 0 and 0 at block 276298961 on 2026-11-05 ([docs/testnet-params.json](testnet-params.json); ADR-18, ADR-20). With both at 0 nothing is charged, so the reserve fund gets no fee and no solver earns one.

**Who runs the keeper and the solver, and who pays them.** The contract lets any account call `close_epoch`, `commit_epoch` and `finalize_epoch` (the keeper steps) and submit a clearing proposal (the solver step); that is what "anyone" means in [architecture.md](architecture.md). The incentive to do it is weak today, and we say so:

- **Keeper: run by the x207 team, unpaid.** No parameter pays a keeper. The team's keeper (the systemd unit `x207-keeper` on the boxd machine `x207`, running `scripts/keeper.mjs` in a loop; see [deploy/boxd](../deploy/boxd/README.md)) moves the epochs of `x207-v013.testnet` until 2026-11-30, normally within a minute of each cutoff. After it is stopped, an epoch waits until some account calls the next step. Members can call the steps themselves, and the cost is the gas of the call only.
- **Solver: not paid in the MVP.** Since epoch 48 `solver_fee_bps` is 0, so a solver earns nothing for a proposal, and cycle clearing does not change what anyone pays (see the README), so it brings no cash saving to pay a solver from. Before that, in epoch 8 the winning proposal cleared 21.8 credit, so the solver fee was 21.8 × `solver_fee_bps` 5 ÷ 10,000 = 0.0109 credit. Its `submit_clearing` burnt 5.16 Tgas and 0.000493 NEAR ([gas-epoch8.json](gas-epoch8.json); the comparison with plain transfers is in [gas-epoch8-vs-transfers.md](gas-epoch8-vs-transfers.md)). If one credit is one USDT, the fee covers that gas only when one NEAR is worth less than about 22 USDT, a break-even computed from these two figures, not from a market price that we checked. It also leaves out the cost to compute the proposal and the gas of a proposal that loses. Nothing in the contract ties the gas of a proposal to a fee, and the fee is bounded by `set_params` at 100 bps. The keeper does not run a solver ([keeper-operations.md](keeper-operations.md)), so an epoch has a clearing only when someone runs a solver, and a third-party solver has no payment reason to do it until the fee is set above 0 again.
- **What this means.** The solver and the keeper are run by the team in this submission. We do not claim that a market of independent solvers exists. [competing-solvers.md](competing-solvers.md) shows that two solvers can compete in one epoch on testnet (UM-123), which is a test of the contract rule, not of an economy.

**Roadmap for incentives.** A keeper reward (a share of the clearing fee or a flat tip per step, paid from the reserve fund and set by the DAO through `set_params`) and a solver fee that follows the gas of the proposal. Neither is built, and no parameter exists for a keeper reward in interface 0.0.13. Today the keeper is paid by the team (the account `x207-v013-aux.testnet` pays the gas), and with the fees at 0 the reserve fund is not fed by new fees.

**Roadmap.** Several circles, one per ecosystem or community, and clearing between circles. Credit above stake with a reserve fund and haircut. Confidential settlement (ADR-15). See [metrics-and-roadmap.md](metrics-and-roadmap.md#10-roadmap).
