Problem: agents that pay per call (x402) settle every call on its own, so a thousand small payments cost a thousand transfers, and the usual fix, a payment channel per seller, needs a deposit per seller, sized before the sellers are known.

What we built: x207, a multilateral clearing layer for x402 agent payments on NEAR. Agents in a circle lock one USDT stake that bounds what they can owe (limit at most the stake) and pay each call with a signed voucher, served at once. At the end of each epoch an open solver finds the debt cycles, one on-chain transaction cancels them, and only what is left is paid in USDT. A default is covered from the stake of the defaulter.

Proof on NEAR testnet (circle x207-v013.testnet, contract 0.0.13): in a realistic epoch, 24 vouchers worth 41.35 credit among 15 participants were cleared by 21.8 credit; the 19.55 left netted to 7.75 USDT due from six debtors; five paid 7.2 USDT and the 0.55 of the one that defaulted was taken from its stake. A complete graph of 210 obligations was cleared in four transactions.

How NEAR fits in: a Rust contract (x207-circle), mock NEP-141 USDT, x402 on NEAR, Sputnik DAO for parameters, NEAR Intents for the residual payment, and an MCP server so an AI agent can read the circle and, with a key it holds, join and pay. The residual batch through the real Intents contract ran only in near-sandbox.

Try it: hosted dashboard, read API and remote MCP server (@x207/mcp 0.0.3, also in the MCP Registry); npm run demo:quick runs it all locally, no account.

Limits: it burns more gas than plain transfers unless a pair trades about 3 to 4 times per epoch, a member locks a stake of at least 125% of its debt, and it helps only where debts go in cycles (measured x402 traffic on Base is mostly star-shaped: arXiv:2607.12575).

Not done: no mainnet transaction (mock USDT; the team's keeper moves epochs until 2026-11-18), NEAR Intents not run on testnet, no real wallet through NEAR Connect, the TEE broker is not built.
